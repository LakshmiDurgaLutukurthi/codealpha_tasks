1. What is Phishing?

Phishing is a social engineering attack where an attacker pretends to be a trusted person or organization to trick a victim into revealing information, opening an attachment, transferring money, or visiting a fake website.

Never enter passwords, OTPs, card details, or other sensitive information just because a message creates urgency or fear.

2. Recognizing Phishing Emails

  • Unexpected urgency: “Act now” or “Your account will be closed.”
  • Suspicious sender: The display name may look familiar but the actual address can be different.
  • Unusual links: Visible link text can hide a different destination.
  • Requests for secrets: Be suspicious of requests for passwords or OTPs.
  • Unexpected attachments: Treat unfamiliar files with caution.
  • Unusual wording: Spelling, grammar, or formatting may be inconsistent.

3. Spotting Fake Websites

Check the domain

Look carefully at the actual domain name, not only the logo or page design.

HTTPS is not enough

HTTPS encrypts traffic, but a padlock alone does not prove that a site is legitimate.

Avoid pressure links

Open the official app or type a known website address yourself when possible.

Check requests

Be cautious if a site suddenly asks for sensitive information it normally does not need.

4. Social Engineering Tactics

TacticHow it worksDefense
UrgencyPressures you to act quickly.Pause and verify.
AuthorityPretends to be a manager, bank, IT team, or official.Verify using a trusted contact method.
FearClaims an account or payment is in danger.Use the official channel instead of the message link.
RewardPromises prizes, refunds, jobs, or offers.Question unexpected rewards.
ImpersonationCopies names, branding, or writing style.Check the actual sender and domain.

5. Real-World-Style Examples

Fake account alert

“Your account will be suspended today. Verify immediately using the link below.”

Red flags: urgency, threat, login link.
Safe action: Open the official website/app directly.

Fake delivery message

“Delivery failed. Pay a small fee now to reschedule.”

Red flags: unexpected payment, short deadline, unfamiliar link.
Safe action: Check the order in the official app/site.

Fake job message

“Congratulations! Pay a registration fee to confirm your job today.”

Red flags: payment request and pressure.
Safe action: Verify the company independently and do not pay simply to secure a job.

6. Best Practices

  1. Stop and think before clicking.
  2. Verify unexpected requests through a separate trusted channel.
  3. Check sender addresses and actual link destinations.
  4. Use multi-factor authentication where available.
  5. Keep your operating system, browser, and security software updated.
  6. Report suspicious messages using your organization's approved process.
  7. If you entered credentials on a suspicious site, change the password through the legitimate site and contact the appropriate support/security team.

8. Key Takeaway

STOP → CHECK → VERIFY → REPORT. Taking a few seconds to verify a message can prevent a major security problem.
